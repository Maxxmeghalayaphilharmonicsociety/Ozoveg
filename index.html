The problem after harvest

Residues on the plate. Losses in the chain.

Vegetables eaten raw or lightly cooked carry residue on the very surface we eat — and from the moment they are cut, they begin to lose water and spoil.

Pesticide residues

Many pesticides are oily and cling to the waxy cuticle of leaves and fruit, resisting a plain tap-water rinse at home.

Microbes

Spoilage organisms ride in on the surface, and uncontrolled wash water can spread them across a whole batch.

Moisture loss & spoilage

In warm climates with fragmented cold chains, a large share of fresh produce wilts, softens or rots before it is sold.

The OZOVEG concept

Clean first, then seal.

Cleaning and protection are two halves of one job. The first stage removes what should not be eaten; the second protects what should.

Stage 1 · CLEAN

Remove residues and microbes

  1. Pre-rinse — remove soil and debris
  2. Oxidative wash — degrade residues, reduce microbes
  3. Potable rinse — carry away oxidant and by-products
Stage 2 · PROTECT

Seal in freshness

  1. Edible coating dip — apply a protective film
  2. Forced-air drying — set the coating evenly
  3. Pack & store — ventilated packs for cool distribution
